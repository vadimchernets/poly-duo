**Bottom line:** No chapter of the 1859 first edition is the answer. Darwin did not coin "survival of the fittest", and the phrase is not in that edition. I read the question as asking where Darwin used it, which is Chapter IV in the 5th (1869) and later editions.

**Facts**
- Herbert Spencer coined the phrase in *Principles of Biology* (1864), after reading Darwin.
- Alfred Russel Wallace suggested in 1866 that Darwin adopt it.
- Darwin first used it in the 5th edition (1869) and kept it in the 6th (1872). He credited Spencer. In Chapter IV he wrote that natural selection is "what Mr. Herbert Spencer has well expressed by the Survival of the Fittest".
- The 1859 edition has Chapter III ("Struggle for Existence") and Chapter IV ("Natural Selection"). Both use only "natural selection" and "struggle for existence".
- The 6th edition's Chapter IV title is "Natural Selection; or the Survival of the Fittest".

**Uncertain:** I haven't checked whether the 1869 text also uses the phrase in Chapter III. I also don't know whether the 5th edition's Chapter IV title already included "or the Survival of the Fittest". I'm sure of that title for the 6th edition.

Confidence: high
