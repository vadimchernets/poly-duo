**The question's premise is wrong: Darwin didn't coin the phrase, and it isn't in the 1859 first edition.** The colleague's answer is correct, and I found no real errors.

- Herbert Spencer coined "survival of the fittest" in *Principles of Biology* (1864). He used it to describe Darwin's "natural selection".
- Alfred Russel Wallace urged Darwin to adopt it in 1866. Darwin first used it in the fifth edition (1869) of the *Origin*. It appears in the text, and the title of Chapter IV became "Natural Selection; or the Survival of the Fittest". The sixth edition keeps that title.
- In the first edition, Chapter IV is just "Natural Selection" and Chapter III is "Struggle for Existence". The phrase does not occur anywhere in that edition.

If you meant where the phrase first appears in the *Origin*, the answer is Chapter IV of the fifth edition (1869).
