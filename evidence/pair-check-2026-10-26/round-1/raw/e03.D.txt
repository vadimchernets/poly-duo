**$7,000** is the 2024 maximum IRA contribution for someone under 50. The colleague's answer is correct.

- **Combined limit:** The $7,000 applies across all your traditional and Roth IRAs together. It is not a separate limit for each account.
- **Compensation cap:** You can't contribute more than your taxable compensation for the year. If you earned less than $7,000, that lower amount is your limit.
- **Roth income limits:** Higher earners may be limited or barred from contributing to a Roth IRA. For 2024 the phase-out starts at $146,000 modified AGI for single filers and $230,000 for married filing jointly.
- **Traditional IRA deductibility:** You can contribute to a traditional IRA at any income. Whether the contribution is tax-deductible can be limited if you or your spouse is covered by a workplace plan.
- **Age 50 and over:** The limit is $8,000, which includes a $1,000 catch-up contribution.
- **Deadline:** You could make 2024 contributions until the 2025 tax filing deadline, April 15, 2025.

Source: IRS, "Retirement topics – IRA contribution limits."
