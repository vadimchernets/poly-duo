**Bottom line: $7,000 for 2024.**

Reading chosen: the combined limit across all of a person's traditional and Roth IRAs, not a limit per account.

**Facts**
- The 2024 IRA contribution limit was $7,000 for people under 50. It was $6,500 in 2023.
- People aged 50 or older could add a $1,000 catch-up, for $8,000 in total.
- The contribution cannot exceed taxable compensation for the year. A person who earned less than $7,000 is limited to what they earned.
- Roth IRA contributions phase out at higher incomes. For 2024 the phase-out was $146,000–$161,000 for single filers and $230,000–$240,000 for married couples filing jointly.
- Traditional IRA deductibility phases out if the person or their spouse is covered by a workplace plan. The thresholds depend on filing status.
- The deadline for 2024 contributions was April 15, 2025.

**Conclusion**
$7,000 is the ceiling. Your actual limit can be lower if your earnings are low or your income falls in a Roth phase-out range.

**Not sure**
I'm confident about the $7,000 and $1,000 figures. I'm quoting the phase-out ranges from memory, so check them against IRS Notice 2023-75 before relying on them.
