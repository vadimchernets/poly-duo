**212 °F** is the boiling point of water at sea level. This is for pure water at standard atmospheric pressure (1 atm, 101.325 kPa), which equals 100 °C.

The colleague's answer is correct. A few details that may help:
- Real sea-level pressure varies with the weather, so the actual boiling point shifts slightly, by about 1 °F or less.
- The boiling point drops by roughly 1 °F for every 500 ft of altitude gained.
- Dissolved solids such as salt raise it slightly.
