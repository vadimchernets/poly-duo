**$14,600** is the federal standard deduction for a single filer for tax year 2024 (returns filed in 2025).

**Facts**
- The IRS set this in Rev. Proc. 2023-34, an inflation adjustment of $450 from 2023's $13,850.
- For comparison, the 2024 amounts for other filing statuses are $29,200 for married filing jointly and $21,900 for head of household. A married person filing separately gets $14,600.
- Extra amounts apply to filers who are 65 or older or blind. For single or head of household filers, each condition adds $1,950. For married filers, each adds $1,550.
- A dependent's deduction is limited to the greater of $1,300 or earned income plus $450, but it can't exceed $14,600.

**Conclusion**
The base figure is $14,600. I'm confident in it.

**Not sure:** I'm not certain of the 2024 add-on amounts for the elderly or blind. I recall them from memory and haven't checked them against the IRS text.

**Reading chosen:** "Single filer" means an unmarried taxpayer who isn't a head of household, and "standard deduction" means the base amount before any age, blindness or dependent adjustments.

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (July 2025) raised the standard deduction starting in tax year 2025. It did not change the 2024 figure.
