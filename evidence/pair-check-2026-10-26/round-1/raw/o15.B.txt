The colleague's answer is correct. I'd add three short points:

- **Conversion:** K = °C + 273.15.
- **Definition:** Since the 2019 SI redefinition, the kelvin is fixed by the Boltzmann constant, not by the triple point of water. The ice point is therefore a measured value, about 273.15 K to within roughly 0.0001 K, and 273.15 K is the conventional value.
- **Triple point:** Don't confuse the freezing point with the triple point of water. The triple point is about 273.16 K, at 611.657 Pa.

I read the question as pure water at 1 atm. Dissolved air or salts lower the freezing point slightly. For rough work, 273 K is fine.
