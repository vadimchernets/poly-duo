**Bottom line: 12 workweeks of unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period.**

Reading chosen: leave for the employee's own serious health condition under 29 U.S.C. § 2612(a)(1)(D), which is the standard FMLA entitlement.

**Facts**
- The entitlement is 12 workweeks in a 12-month period. It is unpaid, though the employee may choose to use accrued paid leave, or the employer may require it, to run concurrently.
- The employee must be eligible:
  - They have worked for the employer for at least 12 months. The months need not be consecutive, and some earlier service can be excluded.
  - They have worked at least 1,250 hours in the 12 months before the leave starts.
  - They work at a site with 50 or more employees within 75 miles.
  - The employer is covered: a private employer with 50 or more employees, or a public agency or school.
- The leave can be taken all at once, intermittently, or on a reduced schedule when medically necessary.
- The employer chooses how to measure the 12-month period: calendar year, fixed fiscal year, rolling period measured forward, or rolling period measured backward.
- The employee is entitled to return to the same job or an equivalent one. Group health coverage continues on the same terms as if they were working.
- A separate entitlement of up to 26 workweeks in a single 12-month period applies to military caregiver leave. It does not apply to the employee's own condition.

**Conclusion**
For one's own serious health condition, the cap is 12 weeks. States may give more through their own laws, such as California and New York, and some of those state programs offer paid leave. That is separate from the federal FMLA.
