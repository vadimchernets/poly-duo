**Bottom line: 3 crossings.**

Reading chosen: the boat holds the farmer plus at most two items, and the classic restrictions apply (wolf+goat and goat+cabbage can't be left alone together; wolf+cabbage is safe).

**Facts**
- One crossing is impossible because all three items can't fit in the boat.
- The number of one-way crossings must be odd, since the farmer ends on the far bank.
- That leaves 3 as the smallest candidate.

**A 3-crossing solution**
1. The farmer takes the wolf and the cabbage across. The goat is alone on the start bank, which is safe.
2. The farmer returns alone. The wolf and cabbage are left together on the far bank, which is safe because the wolf doesn't eat cabbage.
3. The farmer takes the goat across. All three are now on the far bank.

**Conclusion:** the minimum is 3, and it is proven by the lower bound above plus this valid sequence. I'm sure of this, with no ambiguity beyond the reading stated.
